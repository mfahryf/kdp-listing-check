Amazon KDP listing rules on one page

What your title, description, keywords and categories may and may not contain, gathered from KDP's own help pages.

KDP's rules for a book listing are spread across three help pages. None is long, but together they hold a few hard limits and several dozen things to avoid, and it is easy to break one without noticing. This page puts them in one place, in plain language, with examples.

Title and subtitle

The title field is for the title as it appears on your cover, and nothing else. The subtitle is optional and follows the same rules. Together they must come to fewer than 200 characters.

What does not belong in either:

  • Generic words repeated to catch searches, such as "notebook", "journal", "gifts" or "books".
  • Other books, other authors, or trademarks you are not authorised to use.
  • Anything suggesting someone contributed to the book who did not.
  • Sales-rank claims such as "bestselling".
  • Advertising or promotional words such as "free".
  • Punctuation only, or a placeholder such as "unknown", "n/a" or "none".
  • HTML tags.
  • Extra descriptive padding beyond the real title.

Breaks the rules: The Lighthouse Keeper's Daughter: A Bestselling Gripping Historical Mystery Novel Book

Fine: The Lighthouse Keeper's Daughter

The first adds a sales-rank claim and descriptive padding. What kind of book it is belongs in the description, the categories and the keywords.

Two more points. If one book holds several stories, the title should say so with a word like "Collection" or "Compilation". And KDP notes that customers tend to skim past titles longer than 60 characters, which is advice rather than a rule.

Series, author and contributors

  • A series name follows the title rules. Public-domain and low-content books, such as notebooks and journals, are not eligible for series.
  • The author field is for the primary contributor. Pen names are allowed as long as they do not mislead buyers.
  • List only people who actually worked on this book. No keywords and no HTML in these fields.
  • A translation credits both the translator and the original author.

Description

The description may be up to 4,000 characters, and that count includes any HTML tags. A bold word written as <b>test</b> uses 11 characters, not 4. The live counter in KDP's editor counts the same way.

HTML that works

TagUse
<p>Paragraph
<br>Line break
<b>, <i>, <em>, <u>Bold, italic, emphasis, underline
<h4>, <h5>, <h6>Headings; <h4> is the largest
<ul>, <ol> with <li>Bulleted and numbered lists

What does not work: <h1>, <h2> and <h3>; lists without <li> items; several spaces in a row, which are not displayed; and stray angle brackets such as << or <>.

Breaks the rules: <h2>About this book</h2>

Fine: <h4>About this book</h4>

What a description may not contain

  • Website addresses, email addresses, phone numbers or postal addresses.
  • Reviews, quotes or testimonials.
  • Requests for customer reviews.
  • Advertisements or promotional material.
  • Time-sensitive information, such as the dates of a tour or an event.
  • Price, availability or other ordering information.
  • Spoilers.
  • Emoji.
  • Keywords or tag phrases.
  • Pornographic, obscene or offensive content.

Breaks the rules: "I could not put it down" — a reader. Only $0.99 this week! More at my website. Please leave a review!

That one line holds a testimonial, a price, time-sensitive wording, a pointer to a website and a review request. Each is on the list above.

A year or a date that is part of the story, such as "Cornwall, 1887", is not time-sensitive information. A launch date or a sale deadline is.

The most common slip is a list of search phrases at the end of the description. Those belong in the keyword fields.

Keywords

You have up to seven keyword fields. Each can hold a word or a short phrase; KDP's metadata guidelines recommend phrases of two or three words. Put the words in the order a person would type them: "military science fiction", not "fiction science military".

What works

KDP suggests using keywords for what will not fit in your title or description. It names five useful kinds:

  • Setting: the place, period or world.
  • Character types: who the story is about.
  • Character roles: the part they play.
  • Plot themes: what the story turns on.
  • Story tone: how it feels to read.

What is not allowed

  • Other authors, or books by other authors.
  • Sales-rank claims such as "bestselling".
  • Advertising or promotional words such as "free".
  • Anything unrelated to the book.
  • HTML tags.

What to avoid

  • Words already in your title, author name or categories. KDP lists these as information to leave out; use the field for something new.
  • Subjective claims about quality.
  • Time-sensitive words such as "new", "on sale" or "available now".
  • Words common to nearly everything in the store, such as "book".
  • Spelling mistakes.
  • Variants of the same keyword in spacing, punctuation, capitals or plurals.
  • Brands you do not own.
  • Quotation marks.
  • Amazon program names such as "Kindle Unlimited" or "KDP Select".

Wasted or not allowed: best historical mystery book · "cornish mystery" · kindle unlimited mystery · free mystery · victorian smugglers · victorian smuggler

Better: amateur detective · smugglers and wreckers · father and child secrets · nineteenth century england

The first set has a quality claim, the word "book", quotation marks, a program name, a promotional word, and two plural variants of one phrase.

How to test a keyword

No rule tells you whether shoppers actually type a phrase. KDP's own advice is to search Amazon with it before you publish: type it into the search bar, look at the suggestions that appear, then look at the results. If the books on the first page would sit beside yours on a shelf, the phrase fits. If they would not, change it.

Keywords and descriptions can be changed in KDP as often as you like, so nothing here is permanent. If your book is in several formats, keep them consistent across formats.

Categories

You may choose up to three categories. They must be relevant to the book's central storyline. Choosing a category because it looks easier to rank in, when the book does not belong there, is against the rules.

A checklist before you publish

  1. Is the title exactly what is on the cover?
  2. Are the title and subtitle together under 200 characters?
  3. Is the description under 4,000 characters with the HTML counted?
  4. Does the description use only the supported tags?
  5. Is it free of links, prices, dates, reviews, review requests and emoji?
  6. Are there no keyword lists in the description?
  7. Does each of the seven keywords say something your title and categories do not?
  8. Are the keywords free of claims, "book", program names and quotation marks?
  9. Have you searched Amazon for each keyword?
  10. Do all three categories describe the whole book?
